zeta-explorer

The Riemann zeta function, computed in your browser from the formulas: Euler-Maclaurin summation and the functional equation for ζ(s), and the Riemann-Siegel formula with four correction terms for Z(t). Drag a plot to pan it. To zoom, pinch, or click the plot and then scroll.

The critical strip

Hue is the argument of ζ(σ + it), and the shading restarts each time |ζ| doubles. Zeros are the points where every colour meets, and all of them sit on the dashed line σ = 1/2. The trivial zeros at −2, −4, −6 are on the real axis, and the pole at s = 1 is the spot where the colours go round the other way.

 

Zeros on the critical line

On σ = 1/2, ζ(1/2 + it) = e−iθ(t) Z(t) with Z real, so every zero is a sign change of Z. Below t = 9878 the dots come from a table of the first 10,000 zeros; above it they are found live by bisection. Ticks mark Gram points gn, where θ(gn) = nπ. Gram's law says (−1)nZ(gn) > 0; the orange ticks are where it fails.

 

Primes from zeros

Riemann's explicit formula writes the prime-power counting function ψ(x) = Σpk≤x log p as a smooth term minus one wave per zero: ψ(x) = x − Σρ xρ/ρ − log 2π − ½ log(1 − x−2). Each pair of zeros ρ = 1/2 ± iγ adds an oscillation of frequency γ in log x and amplitude √x/|ρ|. Add them one by one and the steps appear at the prime powers.

Dirichlet L-functions

A Dirichlet character χ mod q is a homomorphism (ℤ/qℤ)× → ℂ×, and L(s, χ) = Σ χ(n) n−s. Characters carry Conrey labels χq(n, ·), the same ones the LMFDB uses, and only the primitive ones are listed. For those the completed function (q/π)(s+a)/2 Γ((s+a)/2) L(s, χ), with a = 0 for even χ and 1 for odd, equals ε(χ) times the same thing for the conjugate character at 1 − s, with ε = τ(χ)/(ia√q) a unit built from the Gauss sum. That makes Z(t, χ) = eiθ(t, χ) L(1/2 + it, χ) real, so zeros on the line are again sign changes. Unlike Z(t) for ζ it is not even: Z(−t, χ) is Z(t, χ̄), and a complex character's zeros below the axis are its conjugate's zeros above it.

 

 

Elliptic curves

A curve E: y² + a₁xy + a₃y = x³ + a₂x² + a₄x + a₆ over ℚ has an L-function built from point counts: ap = p + 1 − #E(𝔽p), here from a Legendre-symbol sum over x for each prime. Modularity gives the functional equation, with the conductor N in place of q and a root number ε = ±1, so Z(t) is again real on the line. Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer predict that the order of vanishing at the centre is the rank of E(ℚ): 0 for 11a, 1 for 37a, 2 for 389a and 3 for 5077a, the smallest conductor of each rank. The central zero is the orange dot at t = 0. Only semistable curves (squarefree N) are accepted.

 

 

Zeros near the 1012th

Montgomery conjectured, and Odlyzko's computations confirmed, that high zeros space out like the eigenvalues of a large random Hermitian matrix (GUE).

Every value on this page is computed by the JavaScript in web/js, which is tested against mpmath. The zero table and the statistics at height 1012 come from the Python package in the same repo (a simplified Odlyzko-Schönhage evaluator). github.com/louisbelot/zeta-explorer
